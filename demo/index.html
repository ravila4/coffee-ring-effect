coffee rings — evaporation-driven particle deposition

Each stain is a Monte-Carlo run of Deegan's coffee-ring physics: solute particles advected by the outward capillary flow of a drying droplet whose contact line is pinned, jamming where they reach it. One concentration knob drives the morphology: the ring grows inward per the Appendix of Deegan's 2000 paper, depinning nucleates dry holes that arrest into arch scallops, and severed drops leave nested inner rings. Dilute drops end in free recession — radial spokes, stick-slip arcs, disorganized center dots. Mug-bottom rings wick from a single drip: short reach makes a crescent, long reach a full ring; a hard set-down splashes from the drip point. Energetic spills finger into stars, eject satellites, and dot ligament trails along the flight lines; the dilute ones dry ringless as specks.
